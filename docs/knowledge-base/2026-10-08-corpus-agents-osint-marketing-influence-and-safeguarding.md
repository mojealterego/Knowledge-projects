## OmniCore agent-generated OS
Source: `--AI w Tworzeniu Systemów Operacyjnych-- (1).pdf` (9 pages). The report assigns Cursor as a Rust `no_std` kernel architect, Windsurf/Cascade for Godot/PUI, Devin as an integration/QEMU tester, and proposes `.cursorrules`, RAG against platform documentation, multi-agent coordination, and experimental AI-assisted scheduling. Already owned by P26/P80/P115. These vendors and configuration paths can change; provider compatibility and agent contracts require current validation. No LLM may patch or govern a privileged kernel by prompt alone. Measurable deliverables: signed task graph, code review, QEMU boot proof, static/fuzz/ABI verification, safety rollback and GPU/cost budget.

## Agentic project management and task instructions
Two documents (11 and 15 pages) propose `Y=f(X,W_frozen)` as a planning metaphor; WBS (work breakdown), HTA (hierarchical task analysis), RAG, SOP, ReAct-style feedback, LangGraph and optional MCP adapters. Increasing prompt/context precision does **not** make model output deterministic, and a generated report is not tool-execution evidence. Existing owners P90/P115. Compile every task to `Goal → Scope → Inputs → Capability → Preconditions → Deadline → Action → Verifiable Artifact → Outcome → Recovery` with approval gates, idempotency and recorded tool receipts; protect external e-mail, payment, infra and credential operations.

## OSINT code / simulation provenance
Source: `Agent OSINT.pdf` (4 pages). The source uses Python requests, socket DNS, `http://ip-api.com/json/` without transport encryption, a Chrome browser User-Agent while describing anti-bot bypass, a domain `HEAD` request with redirects, and username checking by HTTP 200/404. These are **risky reference patterns**, not evidence of identity/ownership or authorization for scanning. DNS and banner results can point to shared infrastructure; IP geolocation does not locate a person. HTTP 200 can be a bot gate or generic profile, and HTTP 404 may be privacy control. Separate `simulated`, `observed`, `verified` with original source hashes, timestamps, authority records and non-promoting UI badges. No live OSINT calls executed. The `AETHER.pdf` terminal's `TARGET` command similarly fetches an HTTP IP service and mixes mock neon/crypto/CPU metrics; source already recorded in AETHER KB.

## Psychological influence, coercion and consent
The 73-page Hogan booklet is a copyrighted catalogue of persuasive tactics; its claims of effectiveness are author assertions. The 100-page Grzywa DOC (1997) describes attention, memory, attitudes, ingratation, cognitive dissonance, group pressure, authority, guilt and textual manipulation; existing knowledge covers this. The 405-page scanned dating advice book by Agnieszka Ornatowska contains relationship/dating scenarios, including profile privacy and online safety, but its text extraction has noise and repeated fragments. Do not reproduce tactics as imperceptible influence scripts. P36/P40 gain a **defensive** taxonomy: pressure, identity/authority misuse, guilt leverage, staged urgency, deceptive intimacy and coercive bargaining; explicit user agency, consent and safety review. No diagnosis of a person's mind or reliable "brain control" from textual cues alone.

## Hybrid marketing, offline + online
The 2015 Golik-Górecka paper (22 PDF pages) discusses an expanded Ansoff matrix, virtual market/product development and a hybrid offline/online case study of Company X; its market data refers to 2014, and the conclusions are context-bound. P66 can formalize `channel: online|offline|hybrid`, product vs market novelty, dated evidence, cohort attribution, privacy, margin and falsifiable uplift assumptions. Modern 2026 channel performance, costs and legal rules remain unverified.

## Adult domestic abuse assistance — methodology and safety
Agnieszka Filipek's 2017 doctoral thesis (394 pages) covers forms and consequences of domestic abuse, family/community and institutional help, "Niebieskie Karty", survey methodology and assistance outcomes in selected Podlaskie cities. The **own sample was purposive: n=152**, questionnaire survey conducted in May–September 2016 among adults participating in "Niebieskie Karty" in Białystok (97), Łomża (31) and Suwałki (24). Results should not be generalized to all survivors; legal content predates subsequent changes. The source distinguishes professional social workers, police, psychologists and community support, and notes that appearing compliant with an abuser is not proof of consent. See `docs/knowledge-base/2026-10-08-adult-safeguarding-evidence-boundary.md` and P124.
