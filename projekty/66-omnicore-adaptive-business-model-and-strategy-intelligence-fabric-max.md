# Project 66 — OmniCore Adaptive Business Model & Strategy Intelligence Fabric MAX

## Purpose
A governed system for designing, comparing, testing and continuously revising business models and strategies using evidence, capabilities, economics, market signals and measured outcomes.

## Why this project is new
The supplied corpus contains several overlapping but distinct views: business model as value creation/delivery/capture, strategy as goals and adaptive action, operations as execution architecture, organizational capabilities as strategic assets, and circular economy as ecosystem-level value-flow redesign. This project compiles them into one executable decision-intelligence system rather than another static canvas. fileciteturn124file0L11-L25 fileciteturn124file5L31-L44

## Design principles
### Model ≠ strategy
The system never collapses them into one object. The business model explains value creation, delivery and capture; strategy describes goals, choices and adaptation; the operating model describes execution. The literature recognizes overlap but also meaningful distinctions. fileciteturn124file0L27-L35 fileciteturn124file4L41-L47

### Three-dimensional completeness
Every candidate business model is evaluated through financial, operational and strategic lenses. Kardas summarizes this triad as financial profit generation, internal value-creation processes, and strategic positioning/stakeholder/network choices. fileciteturn124file1L38-L47

### Dynamic adaptation
Strategies and models are versioned because market turbulence, demand changes, technological shifts and supply-side changes can invalidate prior assumptions. fileciteturn124file2L45-L74

### Capability-grounded advantage
A strategic choice must map to organizational capabilities. Siwak emphasizes capabilities as strategically important intangible assets that can be rare, hard to substitute and difficult to copy. fileciteturn124file3L7-L24

### Customer participation
Where appropriate, value design includes structured customer participation rather than assuming the organization already knows the optimal proposition. The hotel-sector material demonstrates this logic through niche positioning and customer involvement in value design. fileciteturn124file7L2-L16

### Circular-system view
Circular opportunities are represented through material/resource loops and stakeholder ecosystems rather than a single-company process. GOZ 2030 emphasizes resource circulation, reduced waste, added value and industrial symbiosis. fileciteturn124file6L51-L62

## Business Model Compiler
Input:
- customer segment hypothesis;
- problem/need;
- value proposition;
- channels;
- relationship model;
- key activities/resources;
- partners;
- revenue logic;
- cost structure;
- capability constraints;
- evidence and uncertainty.

Output:
- normalized `BusinessModelSpec`;
- assumption register;
- value-flow graph;
- capability dependencies;
- unit-economics model;
- experiment candidates;
- risk register;
- decision gates.

The PARP material supports Business Model Canvas as a practical design instrument, but also explicitly includes evaluation, change planning and experimentation; the project therefore treats the canvas as an input representation, not the final model. fileciteturn124file9L5-L19

Each strategic choice carries explicit assumptions and trigger conditions for re-evaluation.

## Experiment Engine
The system converts uncertain business assumptions into bounded experiments:

`ASSUMPTION → METRIC → TEST DESIGN → SAMPLE / SCOPE → STOP RULE → RESULT → UPDATE`

A model is not promoted because it is persuasive; it is promoted after relevant assumptions are tested against measured outcomes.

## Outcome economics
Track both financial and non-financial outcomes:
- revenue;
- gross/contribution margin;
- acquisition and retention economics;
- delivery cost;
- capacity utilization;
- customer outcome;
- operational reliability;
- capability development;
- resource/material effects where relevant.

## Adaptive strategy state
The runtime maintains:
```text
current_model
current_strategy
assumptions
market_signals
evidence_freshness
capability_state
experiment_results
outcome_history
revision_triggers
```

A new model version requires provenance, changed assumptions and a reason for revision.

## Decision gates
- **G0** Problem/evidence definition
- **G1** Customer/value proposition coherence
- **G2** Financial viability
- **G3** Operational feasibility
- **G4** Strategic/capability fit
- **G5** Scenario robustness
- **G6** Experiment authorization
- **G7** Measured outcome validation
- **G8** Drift/reassessment
- **G9** Release/revision approval

## Cognitive-sovereignty constraints
Commercial strategy must not optimize covert persuasion, dependency or vulnerability targeting. Influence-risk controls from the broader OmniCore portfolio remain active. Product growth can optimize clarity, usefulness, accessibility and voluntary adoption, but not hidden behavioral control.

## Integration with existing portfolio
This project strengthens:
- Project 34 — business-model foundry;
- Project 45 — agentic content + commerce;
- Project 46 — Cognisync integration;
- Project 56 — commercialization/outcome economics;
- Projects 60–64 — data, ML, causal and decision intelligence;
- Project 65 — execution/engineering foundation.

## New capability introduced
`Adaptive Business Model Compiler (ABMC)` transforms the existing portfolio from generating business-model documents into maintaining **versioned, experiment-driven strategic systems** whose assumptions, capabilities, economics and outcomes remain observable and revisable.

## Non-goals
- treating a canvas as proof of product-market fit;
- treating forecasts as realized outcomes;
- treating case studies as universal causal evidence;
- claiming competitive advantage without capability/evidence support;
- using strategy automation as autonomous authority over people or markets.
